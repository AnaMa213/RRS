# Digest — D2 Decision-layer architecture and extensibility (round 1)

Round 1. Budget: 15 tool calls, 8 sources touched (4 read in depth, 2 partially readable, 2 failed/blocked — failures reported below as findings, not hidden).

## Claims

**The Intelligent Driver Model computes acceleration from exactly six named parameters — desired velocity v0, safe time headway T, maximum acceleration a, comfortable deceleration b, minimum standstill spacing s0, acceleration exponent δ — via `v̇ = a(1 − (v/v0)^δ − (s*/s)²)` with `s* = s0 + vT + vΔv/(2√(ab))`; typical values v0=30 m/s, T=1.5 s, a=0.73 m/s², b=1.67 m/s², s0=2 m, δ=4.** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_driver_model | Wikipedia, citing Treiber, Hennecke & Helbing, *Congested traffic states in empirical observations and microscopic simulations*, Physical Review E 62(2):1805–1824 | orig. 2000-08-01 | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high (equation and parameter set are established science, cross-confirmed by SUMO and highway-env below) | class: model-parameter

**IDM's parameters are explicitly understood as describing driving style: "model parameters describe the driving style".** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_driver_model | Wikipedia | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: medium (tertiary source wording; the substantive claim is corroborated by SUMO and highway-env exposing these as per-vehicle-type knobs) | class: model-parameter

**MOBIL's politeness factor p is an explicit selfish↔altruistic↔malicious dial, and the documented behavioural bands are: p = 0 "a purely selfish behaviour. Notice that also selfish drivers do not ignore the safety criterion!"; p ∈ (0, 0.5] "a realistic behaviour: Advantages of other drivers have a lower priority, but are not neglected"; p > 1 "a very altruistic behaviour"; p < 0 "a malicious personality who takes pleasure in thwarting other drivers even at the cost of own disadvantages".** — https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_MOBIL.html | traffic-simulation.de (Martin Treiber, co-author of IDM and MOBIL) | undated page, model from Kesting/Treiber/Helbing 2007 | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high | class: model-parameter

**MOBIL's other two knobs are a safety criterion b_safe (the braking deceleration imposed on the new follower must not exceed a limit; typical 4 m/s², must stay under the ~9 m/s² physical max) and a lane-change threshold a_th (typical 0.2 m/s²) whose purpose is to prevent "frantic lane hopping" by requiring the advantage to clear a barrier; plus a keep-right bias Δb (typical 0.2 m/s², European rules only).** — https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_MOBIL.html | traffic-simulation.de (Treiber) | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high | class: model-parameter

**MOBIL is defined as a general lane-change rule layered on top of *any* car-following model: both the utility of a lane and the risk of the change are expressed as longitudinal accelerations computed by the underlying microscopic model, which is what makes the safety and incentive criteria "compact and general".** — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3141/1999-10 (also https://www.mtreiber.de/publications/MOBIL_TRB.pdf) | Kesting, Treiber & Helbing, *General Lane-Changing Model MOBIL for Car-Following Models*, Transportation Research Record 1999 | 2007 | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: medium — the PDF would not decode for me this run (see "could not find"); claim rests on the abstract/summary text, not on my own reading of the body | class: model-parameter

**SUMO exposes driver-personality knobs as plain per-vehicle-type attributes, quoted from the reference: `tau` = "The driver's desired (minimum) time headway" (default 1.0 s); `accel` (default ~2.6 m/s²) and `decel` (~4.5) = acceleration/deceleration ability; `sigma` = "The driver imperfection (0 denotes perfect driving)" (default 0.5, range [0,1]); `minGap` = "Empty space after leader [m]"; `speedFactor` = mean of the individual speed distribution with `speedDev` (default 0.1) its deviation, where "The product of road speed limit and the individual speed factor gives the desired free flow driving speed of a vehicle"; `apparentDecel` = what the follower assumes the leader can do; `emergencyDecel` (default 9.0) = physical max.** — https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles,_Vehicle_Types,_and_Routes.html | Eclipse SUMO official documentation (DLR) | living doc, accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high | class: model-parameter

**SUMO's lane-change layer is likewise five named aggression dials, quoted: `lcStrategic` = "The eagerness for performing strategic lane changing. Higher values result in earlier lane-changing"; `lcCooperative` = "The willingness for performing cooperative lane changing. Lower values result in reduced cooperation" (range 0–1); `lcSpeedGain` = "The eagerness for performing lane changing to gain speed"; `lcKeepRight`; `lcAssertive` = "Willingness to accept lower front and rear gaps on the target lane. The required gap is divided by this value"; `lcImpatience` (range −1..1) = "Dynamic factor for modifying lcAssertive and lcPushy. Impatience acts as a multiplier. At −1 the multiplier is 0.5 and at 1 the multiplier is 1.5".** — https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles,_Vehicle_Types,_and_Routes.html | Eclipse SUMO docs | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high | class: model-parameter

**SUMO also has a junction-level aggression knob: `impatience` (default 0.0) "is used to represent a drivers willingness to impede vehicles with higher priority. At a value of 1 or above, the driver will use any gap that is *safe* in the sense of collision-avoidance even if it means that another vehicle has to brake as hard as it can."** — https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles,_Vehicle_Types,_and_Routes.html | Eclipse SUMO docs | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high | class: model-parameter. **This is the single most directly rage-shaped parameter found this run** — it is literally "how willing is this driver to force others to brake", and it lives at intersections, where a co-op driving game generates most of its drama.

**SUMO's `lcImpatience` is a documented precedent for the exact "emotion as multiplier on a baseline, not a replacement" pattern**: a separate dynamic scalar in [−1,1] that multiplies the agent's static assertiveness parameters by 0.5…1.5 rather than replacing the lane-change logic. — https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles,_Vehicle_Types,_and_Routes.html | Eclipse SUMO docs | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high | class: architecture-pattern

**A production-grade open-source implementation (highway-env, Farama Foundation) implements IDM + MOBIL as a `IDMVehicle` class whose personality is class-level constants: `ACC_MAX = 6.0`, `COMFORT_ACC_MAX = 3.0`, `COMFORT_ACC_MIN = -5.0`, `DISTANCE_WANTED = 10.0` m, `TIME_WANTED = 1.5` s, `DELTA = 4.0` with `DELTA_RANGE = [3.5, 4.5]` for per-vehicle randomization; lateral behaviour uses MOBIL's `POLITENESS`, `LANE_CHANGE_MIN_ACC_GAIN`, `LANE_CHANGE_MAX_BRAKING_IMPOSED`. It ships named subclasses `AggressiveVehicle` and `DefensiveVehicle`.** — https://highway-env.farama.org/dynamics/vehicle/behavior/ | Farama Foundation, official highway-env docs | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: high for the IDM constants and the existence of the Aggressive/Defensive subclasses; **low/unverified for the aggressive-vs-defensive numeric values, which I could not retrieve** | class: extension-seam

**Utility-based AI scores each candidate action from a set of "considerations", each of which maps a normalized world input through a response curve to a score, and the scores are combined (multiplicatively, with a documented compensation factor because "as more are added, the decision score steadily approaches 0"); adding a new action means adding scorers, not editing existing actions.** — https://www.gameai.com/iaus.php (Dave Mark / Intrinsic Algorithm, Infinite Axis Utility System) and https://uintel-go.utilityworlds.com/Documentation/UtilityIntelligence/Considerations/ (Utility Intelligence, a Unity utility-AI framework) | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: medium — both reached via search-result summaries; the Unity framework's own docs page returned **HTTP 403** to me, so I did not read it directly | class: architecture-pattern

**Practitioner register in the game-AI press holds that behaviour trees answer "should I execute this node?" one node at a time, which is predictable and can be sub-optimal, whereas utility AI computes normalized scores across all options and selects the best — and that "adding personality traits to bias and transform the utility model" is a natural affordance of the latter.** — https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/are-behavior-trees-a-thing-of-the-past- and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/indie-ai-programming-from-behaviour-trees-to-utility-ai | Game Developer (formerly Gamasutra) | dates not captured this run | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: **low — this is advocacy register from utility-AI proponents, not a production post-mortem with numbers.** Treat as hypothesis, not finding. | class: architecture-pattern

**Hybrids are a documented real option: a "Goal-Oriented Behavior Tree" framework integrates utility-theory scoring into conventional behaviour trees, applying BT control flow while using utility-based action selection where dynamic choice is needed.** — https://www.jmis.org/archive/view_article?pid=jmis-10-4-321 | Journal of Multimedia Information Systems, vol 10 no 4 | 2023 | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: low (title/abstract only; not read in full) | class: architecture-pattern

**The best-known open Unity traffic sample (mchrbn/unity-traffic-simulation) is waypoint-based with two scripts doing the work — `WheelDrive` (torque, max speed) and `VehicleAI` (steering/navigation, forward raycast for collision detection) — with parameters exposed as inspector fields, and is documented as "Developed / Tested with Unity 2018.3.x and plus".** — https://github.com/mchrbn/unity-traffic-simulation | GitHub README | accessed 2026-09-15 | confidence: medium for the component split (README-level only); **I did not read the C# source, so I cannot state whether the decisions are in virtual/overridable methods** | class: extension-seam

## Architecture comparison: how hard is it to inject a modifier layer

Ranked by evidence gathered this run, best to worst for RRS's stated requirement:

1. **Parameterized microscopic model (IDM + MOBIL), personality = a struct of ~9 floats.** Evidence is overwhelming and primary: the parameters *are* the personality, the negative-politeness band literally names malicious driving, and SUMO ships a dynamic `lcImpatience` multiplier on top of the static constants, which is the exact "emotion modulates, doesn't replace" architecture the brief asks for. Injecting a modifier layer is a function `effective = base * f(rage, fear)` — no architectural change at all. Cost: it does not by itself give you *target selection* (chasing the player who provoked you); that stays a separate layer above.
2. **Utility AI over a consideration set.** Structurally the right home for target choice and discrete decisions ("ram him", "block him", "flee", "normal driving"), because rage becomes a weight/curve modifier rather than new nodes. Evidence quality here is weaker than for IDM/MOBIL — I could only reach it through search summaries and one 403 — and the "BTs are obsolete" framing I found is advocacy, not a post-mortem.
3. **Hybrid BT-with-utility-selectors.** Documented in the literature (GOBT, 2023) as a real middle path; unverified in production.
4. **Monolithic FSM in one MonoBehaviour** (the shape of most free Unity waypoint traffic samples). Every emotional variant is a new state or a new branch inside existing states; combinatorial growth. This is the architecture the brief is right to fear — and it is what the README-level evidence for the common Unity samples suggests (`VehicleAI` doing steering + navigation + raycast collision in one component).

The load-bearing consequence for the build/adapt/reference decision: **the decision layer RRS needs is roughly 9 floats and one multiplier function.** That is small enough that "adapt an existing Unity traffic system" is only attractive if that system's speed/gap/lane decisions are already expressed as per-vehicle parameters. If its driving logic is a waypoint follower with hardcoded thresholds, adapting it means writing the IDM/MOBIL layer anyway *and* fighting the existing one.
