- (capability) CAP-1 Cooperative chaos loop: players drive as a chaotic road-trip crew that turns bad drivers into incidents, money, and progression; success is a two-player run where driving, provocation, rage escalation, crisis, reward, and upgrade form one loop.
- (capability) CAP-2 Active passenger play: passengers can provoke, threaten, throw, hinder, collect, help, lightly sabotage, and amplify incidents; success is three MVP passenger actions that visibly alter rage or incident state.
- (capability) CAP-3 Per-vehicle rage: each enemy vehicle tracks its own rage and behavior instead of using a global meter; success is three AI vehicles independently escalating or staying calm in one route.
- (capability) CAP-4 Rage Road confrontations: rage escalation can create a crisis where drivers flee, block, ram, or force an on-foot fight; success is one Rage Road event that players can trigger and resolve for money.
- (capability) CAP-5 Road-rage economy: meaningful money comes mainly from provoking, confronting, and beating hostile drivers, while absurd actions pay little and mostly trigger play; success is earning one reward from road rage and buying one upgrade.
- (capability) CAP-6 On-foot sandbox support: on-foot play supports upgrades, trash collection, Rage Road fights, absurd interactions, and compact stop zones; success is a simple transition that returns value to the same rage-money-upgrade loop.
- (constraint) MVP scope is one route, one player car, two players maximum, three AI vehicles, three passenger actions, simple on-foot transition, one compact sandbox zone, one money reward, one upgrade, and one Rage Road event.
- (constraint) On-foot and in-car modes must feed the same rage, money, upgrades, and preparation loop so they do not become two separate games.
- (constraint) Significant currency must come from road-rage confrontation; absurd sandbox actions are low-value toys and escalation triggers.
- (constraint) Cities and stops are sandbox opportunity zones with happenings, money opportunities, shops, and incidents, but are not destructible city simulations.
- (constraint) Prototype priorities must prove the irreducible cooperative driving, passenger provocation, per-vehicle rage, crisis, and money loop before boss, roguelite, deep economy, weapon variety, or asset breadth.
- (decision) Defer rich city content, complex final boss, roguelite progression, many archetypes, deep economy, varied weapons, four-player multiplayer, and large 3D asset volume outside MVP.
- (assumption) Assume the first downstream target is a solo-beginner-feasible 3D prototype, because the Forge goal framed feasibility that way.
